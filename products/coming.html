Not yet available

Four products you cannot buy yet

Two are built and close. Two are still being written. None of them is on the price list as though you could order it today, and none of them gets a page pretending otherwise — because a roadmap dressed as a product catalogue is how a site stops being worth reading.

See what is available today Prices, including these

Why they are priced anyway. ConditionalAccessPortal and CompliancePortal carry published figures. That is deliberate: the number is settled before they ship rather than negotiated after, so nobody arrives at a renewal to find the thing they were promised now costs whatever the market will bear. Dredd and PosturePortal carry no list price, which is equally deliberate — a price against something still being built is a guess with a currency symbol on it.

Built · close

ConditionalAccessPortal

Your Conditional Access policies, and what they actually let through.

Conditional Access is the front door to a Microsoft 365 tenant, and almost nobody can say with confidence what their own policy set permits. It inventories every policy — enabled, disabled and report-only — checks five named coverage baselines, and draws the paths that actually reach your resources across principals, device classes, policies and resources.

The part worth waiting for is the provenance. Every edge of that map says how much authority stands behind it: computed by us, confirmed against Microsoft's own What-If evaluation, or unconfirmable because we could not sample honestly. Where our evaluator and Entra's disagree, you get told — rather than the map quietly correcting itself and hiding that it had been wrong.

Write-back is included and approval-gated: enable, disable or move a policy to report-only, one approved change at a time. What it will cost.

What it will not do on day one

Assessments run when you ask. There is no recurring schedule yet, so "continuous" is a word used about ShareCare and MailTrust and deliberately not about this.

Its findings stay inside the product — they do not reach PosturePortal rollups, and CompliancePortal reads your policies itself rather than taking our verdict.

Confirmation catches disagreement in one direction: we check the paths we drew. A policy Microsoft applies where we drew nothing is an access path the map does not show, and we do not detect it yet.

Built · close

CompliancePortal

Controls proven by scans, not screenshots.

It maps the findings the other products already produce onto the frameworks you are working against, and keeps that evidence current. The pitch is narrow and worth stating precisely: an auditor does not want a policy document saying MFA is required, they want proof it was enforced across a period, with the exceptions named and time-boxed.

Two axes rather than one. Scope is how many frameworks you need. Depth is whether you want sign-off and automatic capture. Those used to share a number, which meant a company doing SOC 2 alone had to buy every regime in the catalogue to reach attestation. The bands.

Providers get pooled framework licences: a framework is licensed once across the whole book rather than sixty times, because sixty clients paying per tenant is not a price, it is a decline.

The boundary, stated plainly

It covers the controls that map to a technical implementation on the platforms SeQontrol connects to. It is not a compliance programme: it will not run your policy management, your training records or your vendor reviews, and it does not replace the GRC platform that does.

Assessments are on-demand. What is continuous is the evidence underneath — the other products scan on their own schedules, and their control-tagged findings are what an assessment reuses.

Still being written

Further out

Real work is still happening on these. They are described here so the roadmap is legible, not so they can be sold.

Dredd

Not "this is unwise" — "this is not what you approved." Declare or capture your approved configuration, and every unapproved change becomes a finding with exactly two answers: revert it, or ratify it into the baseline. Configuration governance rather than posture scoring.

It will be quoted rather than listed. Its unit is monitored configuration scope, which is the metric this pricing model understands least, and it is being set against real estates rather than guessed.

PosturePortal

The "so what?" layer. A read-only board aggregating findings, risk and coverage from every product into one view, per tenant and across a whole fleet — for the person who has to answer "how are we doing" without opening five products.

It connects to nothing and reads the shared findings store, so it costs almost nothing to run and will not be a separate line. Exactly how it is packaged is unsettled, which is why it carries no price.

How to read a date we have not given you

There isn't one, and that is on purpose. "Built and close" means the code runs and the gaps above are what is left; "still being written" means real work remains. Neither is a commitment to a month, and a roadmap page that invented one would be the first thing on this site you could catch us out on. Ask, and you will get an honest read on where a product actually is.

Start with what feeds it

The board is only as good as what feeds it. Every product writes into the same findings store, so the more of them you run, the more the board is worth — and the fastest way to see that is to put real findings in it.
