What CompliancePortal is — and is not

It covers the technical implementation of controls on the platforms SeQontrol supports. Where a control can be proven by inspecting the configuration of a connected platform, CompliancePortal proves it, continuously, with evidence an auditor can inspect.

It is not a whole-company compliance programme, and we will not sell it as one. It does not cover:

  • Controls outside the platforms it connects to — anything on-premises, in an unsupported SaaS product, or in a system we cannot read
  • Your policies, procedures, training records, HR and joiner-mover-leaver processes
  • Physical and environmental security, business continuity exercises, vendor and third-party management
  • The signed audit opinion — your auditor still issues that; this makes their job cheap and fast

The honest framing: CompliancePortal covers the part of your framework that lives in configuration, and it covers that part continuously. The rest is still yours — and the product tells you plainly which controls it could assess and which it could not, rather than quietly scoring them as passed.

A waiver changes the report, not the estate

Security stands above compliance here, and CompliancePortal is built to respect that rather than work around it. Every framework has an exception process, and a waived control turns a red row green without anything about the tenant changing. Lean on that often enough and the compliance report becomes a documented, signed-off account of a safety you do not have.

So the underlying security finding never disappears. It stays visible in the product and on the board, it stays scored, and the exception that covers it carries a mandatory expiry and is invalidated automatically when the finding changes shape. You can accept a risk deliberately. You cannot use compliance to stop seeing it. More on the principle.

What it does

  • A catalog of 24 frameworks — SOC 2, ISO 27001, NIST CSF, PCI DSS, HIPAA, GDPR, NIS 2, DORA, FedRAMP, CMMC and more — plus a first-party set of our own for the controls the standard frameworks under-specify. See the coverage per framework, including how much of each is provable without a human.
  • Multi-framework crosswalk, so one piece of evidence satisfies overlapping controls in several frameworks at once instead of being collected three times.
  • Automated control probes across the connected technology planes — Entra ID, Exchange Online, SharePoint, Teams, Intune, Azure, Purview, Power Platform, Power BI, GitHub and Azure DevOps — assessing configuration controls with no human effort.
  • Beyond Microsoft — Google Cloud and Amazon Web Services posture through read-only connectors, so a mixed estate is evidenced from one place.
  • Evidence reuse — control-tagged findings from SecurityPortal, ShareCare and MailTrust become control evidence automatically. The automation comes from reusing detection, not rebuilding it.
  • Assessment workflow — pick a framework, assess, drill into control status, with an immutable snapshot trail showing how controls operated over time.
  • Attestation for the human controls — with four-eyes sign-off and a time-to-live, so an attestation cannot silently outlive its truth.
  • Auditor access, time-boxed — scoped, expiring access for an external auditor, plus provided-by-client evidence requests, control ownership and remediation tasks.

Why it matters

Compliance is expensive, manual and point-in-time. Teams collect screenshots into a spreadsheet for the annual audit, then let the evidence rot for eleven months. Regulators and auditors increasingly expect controls to be shown operating, and frameworks keep multiplying with heavy overlap between them.

The automated compliance platforms answer the workflow half well — questionnaires, task tracking, auditor networks — but they are typically shallow on deep Microsoft 365 telemetry. They cannot prove, at control granularity, that external sharing is contained or that Conditional Access coverage actually holds. That is exactly the half SeQontrol already measures.

What we automate, and what we do not

Coverage of the controls that can be automated is high, and the gaps are documented rather than faked. Controls that need a human decision are honest manual attestations with four-eyes and an expiry. Auditors respect that far more than a fabricated automatic pass — and so should you.

Objections, answered straight

"We already use a compliance automation platform."

They are good at workflow and auditor relationships. Ask whether they can prove your Microsoft 365 external-sharing and Conditional Access controls at evidence granularity. In many deals the two are complementary rather than competing.

"Does this replace our auditor?"

No. It makes the audit cheap by handing the auditor continuous, scoped evidence for the technical controls. The opinion is still theirs to sign.

"How much is really automated?"

Most of what can be automated on the connected platforms. The rest is manual attestation with four-eyes and a time-to-live, clearly labelled as such.

"We run five frameworks — isn't that duplicated effort?"

That is what the crosswalk is for. One piece of evidence satisfies the overlapping controls across every framework in scope.

How to read these numbers

“Provable from configuration” means the control is assessed by inspecting a connected platform, rather than by someone signing a statement. It is a property of the control, not a promise that every one of them is implemented today — coverage of that set is high but not complete, and the product reports per-control status rather than a headline percentage.

A low share is not a weakness in the framework or in us. GDPR sits at 2 of 91 because GDPR is mostly lawful basis, records and process. Reporting it as anything else would be the fabricated automation we refuse to ship.
