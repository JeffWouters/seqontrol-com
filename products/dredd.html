Products/Dredd

Configuration drift governance

Not "this is unwise". "This is not what you approved."

Declare or capture your approved configuration, and Dredd catches and governs every unapproved change. Each deviation becomes a finding with exactly two answers: revert it, or ratify it — and both are versioned, attributed and kept.

In one line: nothing changes without being caught, and nothing stays changed without being approved.
Request a baseline walkthrough Licensing

Dredd is deliberately not a best-practice product

A drift finding never means "this setting is unwise" — that is SecurityPortal's job, and CompliancePortal's. A drift finding means one thing only: this is not what you approved. That makes Dredd the change-control layer, and the approved-state trail — who changed what, who ratified it — is audit evidence in its own right.

What it does

  • Versioned approved baselines — declarative controls, with no scripting, describing the configuration your organisation actually approved. Author them directly, promote them from a failing security check, or instantiate them from a template.
  • Continuous and event-driven detection — scheduled scans plus change notifications evaluate reality against the baseline. Every deviation is a fingerprinted finding, so the same drift does not re-alert as something new.
  • Governance, not suppression — waivers and risk acceptances carry a mandatory expiry and are invalidated automatically when the drift changes shape. Waived drift is still drift, and the product never hides that.
  • Approval-gated healing — restore the approved state, never arbitrary automation. Automatic healing sits behind an anomaly gate that holds correlated mass-drift bursts for a human, because a hundred simultaneous changes is a signal, not a queue.
  • Fleet control — push baseline templates to managed tenants, watch one fleet drift board, and bulk-heal across tenants with per-tenant guardrails and approvals.
  • Security bridge — promote a recurring security check failure into an enforced Dredd control. Stop re-finding it; enforce the fix.

Why it matters

Tenant configuration changes constantly — administrators, scripts, vendors and Microsoft itself all touch it — and most organisations cannot answer two basic questions: what changed since we approved this, and who ratified it? Security tooling tells you whether a setting is wise. Nothing owns whether it is what you agreed to.

A guest-access policy quietly widened. A Conditional Access policy edited during an incident and never put back. A consent setting flipped by a well-meaning administrator. Each is invisible until it becomes an audit finding or a breach path.

Where Dredd actually is

It runs, and it is the youngest product here. The governance pipeline is built and merged, the write path is proven live against a real Entra tenant, and the governance, remediation and bulk-heal paths are shipped. Entra ID is the first control plane; additional planes are designed, not shipped. What is still being set is the licence shape — see licensing — so it is quoted rather than listed.

Baseline-from-current-state is designed and not yet built. That is the fastest onboarding path — capture today's configuration as the approved baseline in one action — and it is the next thing to ship. Until it does, baselines are authored directly, promoted from a failing security check, or instantiated from a template. This is the youngest product in the suite and it is scoped that way.

Objections, answered straight

"Our security scanner already checks these settings."

It scores them against someone else's opinion. Dredd enforces your approved configuration and keeps the ratification trail. Different question — and the security bridge turns a recurring failure into an enforced control.

"We do change control through tickets."

Tickets record intent. Dredd verifies reality. Every change that skipped the ticket shows up as drift, and every ticketed change gets ratified into the baseline with attribution.

"Will it fight our administrators?"

No. "Approve into baseline" carries the same weight as revert, detect-only is the default, and automatic healing holds mass changes for a person. Dredd forces the decision, not the rollback.

"This looks early."

It is, and we say so. The governance pipeline is built and the Entra write path is proven live. The scope is deliberately narrow — one control plane done properly rather than five claimed loosely.
